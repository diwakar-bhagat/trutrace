The moment that matters

The call is the contract.

When a customer disputes an invoice, the support call is where the company's position gets set: what was metered, what the total is, what was promised. If an AI agent summarises that call and states a number the call never established, the company has not saved a support ticket. It has manufactured the next dispute.

The claims that fail

Two of them are money.

Invoice total: claimed $1,540. Verdict: contradictory.

The call states two prices: $1,540 of usage before the credit, and $1,240, the invoice total. The agent wrote down the wrong one as the total. TruTrace asserts neither and shows both, because a billing agent that picks the louder number has just told a customer they owe $300 more than they do.

Credit balance: claimed $500. Verdict: no check.

Support says, in terms, that the remaining balance is not in front of them and not on the invoice. The agent states $500 anyway. TruTrace has no balance check today, so it marks the claim unchecked rather than letting it pass as verified. An unchecked label is honest; a silent pass would be the failure.

Advanced analytics entitlement: claimed as included. Verdict: unsupported.

Support hedges and refuses to promise it: “usually included, I believe, I would need to double-check.” The extraction asserts it as included. An entitlement stated stronger than it was said is a feature the customer now believes they bought.

Stated limits

  • The events count is not established. The agent's figure of 1,842,300 events is stated plainly in the call, and the current verification profile, built on seller calls, does not ground a metered-event count. It reports the claim unsupported rather than guessing. A billing profile, events, rates, balances, entitlements, is the named next piece of work.
  • The overage rate has no check either. $0.40 per thousand events is stated in the call and claimed correctly, and it still passes only as unchecked, because no check exists for it yet.
  • The call is a scenario. Written for this page, not a recording, and labelled as one wherever it appears.
  • One run, not a benchmark. The verdicts above are a single recorded run against this scenario. The measured benchmark, 32 calls and a 10 call campaign, lives on the main page, scored against gold labels drafted by an AI and pending human review.

The fit

Billing is a trust product. This is the trust layer.

Flexprice sells the numbers a company charges by. Every one of those numbers that an agent later states to a customer, in support, in docs, in an AI coworker, either carries the line it rests on or it is a dispute waiting to happen. TruTrace is that proof layer: it sits behind the agents Flexprice and its customers already run, and it refuses to let a claim travel further than its evidence. The verification discipline is built and measured. The billing profile is small, named work, and it is the work I want to do with your team.
